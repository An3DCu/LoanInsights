About

Loan Insights works out loan payments, compares two loans and shows what paying extra saves.

How the math works

Every payment is the standard annuity formula, the same one banks and spreadsheet PMT() functions use:

payment = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)−n)

Here P is the amount borrowed, r is the yearly rate divided by the number of payments in a year, and n is the number of payments. With no interest, the payment is simply P / n.

Rounding

Every line of the schedule is rounded to the cent, the way a bank statement is. The small differences that rounding leaves behind all go into the last payment, so the balance ends at exactly 0.00.

Bi-weekly payments

There are two kinds, and they are not the same thing:

  • Bi-weekly (Calculator and Compare): 26 payments a year, each at a 26th of the yearly rate, over the same term.
  • Accelerated bi-weekly (Pay off faster): pay half your monthly payment every two weeks. That's 26 halves, or 13 full payments a year instead of 12, so the loan ends years earlier.

Currencies

The currency menu changes how amounts are written (€, KM, $ or £), never the math. There are no exchange rates here: 10,000 stays 10,000.

Your data

  • Nothing you type leaves your browser. There are no accounts, no tracking and no server doing the math.
  • Two settings are kept on this device so they're still there next time: light or dark, and the currency.
  • After a result, the page address holds your numbers, so it can be shared. Anyone you send that link to sees those numbers.
  • CSV files are made in your browser and go straight to your downloads.

How it's built

Piece Choice Why
Pages One page plus HTML fragments, swapped in by a small hash router I wrote on jQuery Works on any static host, and back, forward and refresh keep you on the right page
DOM and events jQuery 4 Event delegation keeps the forms working each time a page is swapped back in
Look Bootstrap 5.3, built from Sass with my own variables The savings book theme comes from Bootstrap's own variables, and only the parts in use are built
The math A plain JavaScript module, tested in Node No page and no jQuery in it, so every number can be tested on its own
Charts Chart.js 4 Loaded only when a chart is drawn, so the other pages stay light
Fonts IBM Plex Sans and Mono, Caveat Served from this site, so the page makes no outside requests
